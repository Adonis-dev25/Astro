Healthy Boundaries

Boundaries are not walls. They are the lines that tell your partner what you are comfortable with, so love can feel safe instead of stressful.

Key Ideas

Emotional

Your feelings are valid. You do not have to accept being shouted at or belittled.

Physical

Your body, your choice. A yes today is not a yes forever.

Time and space

Time with friends, family and alone is healthy, not a betrayal.

Digital

Passwords, messages and photos are private unless you choose to share.

Do and Avoid

Do

  • State your limits calmly and clearly
  • Respect a "no" the first time
  • Accept that boundaries can change
  • Keep your own friends and interests

Avoid

  • Guilt-trip someone for saying no
  • Demand constant location or access
  • Make threats to get your way
  • Ignore your own discomfort

Try This

  1. Notice what makes you uncomfortable.
  2. Decide what you need.
  3. Say it simply: "I need..." or "I am not okay with..."
  4. Follow through if it is crossed.
"Boundaries are a way of saying: this is how you can love me well."
- Common saying

Common Challenges

Feeling guilty

Guilt does not mean you did wrong. It often shows up when you start putting yourself first.

Partner pushes back

Repeated disrespect of your limits is a warning sign. Talk to someone you trust.

Control disguised as love

Constant monitoring, isolation from friends or threats are not love. Seek help from a trusted adult or counsellor.
